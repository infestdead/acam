A live viewer for your own RTSP camera, or a spare phone used as one, built for watching a sleeping baby.

<b>Movement highlight</b> — pixels that are slowly changing are tinted, so the rise and fall of a sleeping child's chest shows up even when it is too small to notice in the picture.

<b>Sound line</b> — a scrolling line of the sound level, measured against the room's own background noise. Works with the speaker muted.

<b>Movement line</b> — a second line on the same graph shows how much of the picture is moving, so you can set the alarm level against it.

<b>Visual alarms</b> — the screen pulses red when sound or movement passes a level you set. Each alarm has its own switch and sensitivity.

<b>Liveness</b> — a clock that only advances while frames are actually arriving, and a red badge the moment they stop.

<b>A spare phone as the camera</b> — for travelling: camera mode turns a second Android phone into the camera, found on the same Wi-Fi or reached over a VPN. From the watching phone you can switch between its back and selfie camera and turn on its torch, or use its screen as a light.

Low latency, pinch to zoom, several cameras. No account, no cloud, no analytics: the app talks to your camera and to nothing else.

Needs a camera or recorder with an RTSP stream (rtsp:// or rtsps://), or a second phone in camera mode; H.264 or H.265 video; sound is tested with AAC audio. Encrypted streams use trust on first use for the camera's certificate.

ACam is not a safety or medical device. It does not measure breathing and must not be relied on to detect that a child is unwell or in danger. The alarms pulse a red glow around the edge of the screen; switch them off if anyone who may see it is sensitive to flashing light.
